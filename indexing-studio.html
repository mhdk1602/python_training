Why the curves matter

Locality is a fractal property.

A space-filling curve maps the 2D grid to a 1D order. Hilbert and Z-order are both fractals: the curve at scale 2N is built by recursively placing four scaled copies of the curve at scale N. That recursion is what gives them their locality. Two cells that are close in the grid land close on the curve, on average. Storage engines cash that property in as fewer pages read per query.

Why HNSW belongs here

The vector index is a small-world graph.

HNSW assigns each point to a layer with exponentially decaying probability. The result is a hierarchy with fractal degree distribution and logarithmic search complexity. The same Watts-Strogatz, Barabasi, and Song-Havlin-Makse machinery applies. Vector databases ship HNSW as a black box; this lab opens it.
